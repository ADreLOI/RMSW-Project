## First resolve the connection between Section 1 and Section 2

Your existing idea compares communication-agnostic, reactive and predictive approaches to cooperative seabed mapping. Before writing methods, both authors need to answer:

1. Are the vehicles surface boats (USVs), not underwater vehicles (AUVs)? Mapping the seabed does not mean the vehicle or communication link is underwater.
2. Is the link boat-to-boat, boat-to-shore, or relayed? Choose one initial architecture and draw the information flow yourselves.
3. What is exchanged: coverage updates, task assignments, positions, or raw sensor data? Which decisions cannot be made with stale information?
4. What exactly happens after a missed message in the reactive baseline? What observation triggers an action? What changes when prediction is enabled?
5. What is the predicted event, prediction horizon and available input? How does the prediction affect a decision before an outage?
6. Which one outcome is primary, and what cost or constraint prevents a trivial improvement?

Do not assume that disconnected boats necessarily duplicate coverage. If disjoint areas and routes are assigned before launch, each boat can continue its own survey without communication. Use this as a sanity check and a meaningful baseline. If your intended system reallocates tasks online, explain why, and document how stale state affects that actual algorithm. Do not weaken the baseline solely to make prediction look useful.

Keep the research question neutral about the outcome. Use the mentor's comparison structure: specified strategy X versus specified strategy Y, under specified conditions, evaluated by metric Z. Do not claim a novel predictor or a gap until the closest papers support that claim.

## 2.1 Research Design and Methodological Rationale

**Purpose:** explain how your method can answer the question and why it is feasible.

Questions to answer in your own draft:

- Why is a controlled quantitative simulation appropriate for the comparison you chose?
- Which mechanism is being isolated, and which components remain identical across strategies?
- What would a real-boat experiment add? Why is it unavailable or outside your resources?
- Could existing simulation code supply the needed model more reliably than implementing it from scratch?
- What conclusions will remain limited to the modeled conditions?

Address Andrea's realism concern explicitly. Software correctness, model fidelity and validity are different. Tests help establish that the program implements the model; they do not establish that the model represents a real maritime link. A complex simulator with poorly justified parameters is not automatically more valid. Conversely, a simple simulator can omit a phenomenon that reverses the comparison.

List the omissions most likely to change your conclusion. Then choose a targeted sensitivity check. Full fluid dynamics, detailed sonar reconstruction, underwater acoustics, a new routing protocol and a learned controller should not all become requirements of one small project. A 2D motion model still needs meaningful speed and turning assumptions if travel time is measured.

## 2.2 Data, Materials, Participants, or Other Sources

**Purpose:** identify the evidence, its origin and its limitations.

Specify and justify:

- Initial number of vehicles, area geometry, routes, survey footprint, positioning assumptions and motion constraints.
- A precise meaning of "coverage": surveyed area is not the same as bathymetric reconstruction accuracy.
- Link model, units, update frequency, delay/loss process, and the origin of each parameter (measurement, paper, or explicit synthetic assumption).
- Inputs actually available to each strategy at each time. The predictor cannot see future outage draws or global simulator state unavailable to the boats.
- Data logs required to recover the primary metric, message overhead, failures and relevant secondary measures.
- Reused software: repository URL, license, pinned revision, modifications and a test reproducing an upstream example.

If stochastic losses are independent of all observed inputs, useful outage prediction may be impossible. If outages are entirely determined by future simulator state exposed to the predictor, the comparison becomes unfair. Explain what makes the proposed prediction both plausible and imperfect. An oracle with future knowledge, if used, is a diagnostic comparator, not a deployable strategy or guaranteed upper bound without proof.

No human-participant study is implied by the present simulation idea. Keep meeting recordings, transcripts and others' personal information outside the repository. Do not redistribute downloaded papers unless their license allows it.

## 2.3 Work Procedure and Data Collection

**Purpose:** show a sequence you can finish and reproduce, not a list of tools.

Use this checklist to write your own procedure:

1. Agree on the question, baselines, primary measure and initial scenario; extract supporting model assumptions from the closest papers.
2. Try an existing implementation where relevant; record setup time and a reproducible example. Set a short time budget before committing to a large stack.
3. Verify the simplest case: one boat, then two boats with fixed disjoint assignments; test reliable and disconnected communication. Record what actually depends on communication.
4. Implement or reproduce the chosen coordination mechanism and its reactive behavior. Inspect trajectories, exchanged state and coverage before enabling prediction.
5. Add only the agreed predictive intervention. Keep non-predictive components and available observations comparable. If it changes message frequency or travel, measure the cost or enforce the same budget.
6. Run a small pilot to assess runtime, variance, failures and whether the logs can produce the planned graphs. Freeze the final parameter sweep and analysis choices before the main runs.
7. Use independent runs as repetitions. Where meaningful, pair strategies on identical initial conditions and exogenous disturbances. Use separate random streams so a strategy's extra random calls do not silently change the disturbance realization.
8. Save configurations, seeds, code revision, package versions, raw logs and analysis scripts. Keep tuning/development scenarios separate from final evaluation scenarios.

Choose repetition count using pilot variability, desired precision and available runtime; "30 seeds" is not a course requirement. Do not treat every simulated time step as an independent experimental repetition.

Write your own time estimates and fallback. If prediction does not help, report and explain that finding; do not keep adjusting the scenario until it wins. Discuss scope changes with the mentor if the original question proves unanswerable. Higher-fidelity checks are optional only when the core question can already be answered responsibly.

## 2.4 Planned Data Analysis

**Purpose:** connect each question to an observable comparison and a graph.

Fill in this table before writing prose:

| Item | Author decision needed |
| --- | --- |
| Primary metric | Exact definition, units, measurement start and end |
| Completion | Survey threshold, timeout, whether return/upload is part of completion |
| Primary comparison | Which two policies; what is held constant |
| Independent variable | A justified range of one main link/scenario parameter |
| Main figure | X-axis, Y-axis, policy curves, uncertainty intervals |
| Cost/trade-off figure | Message count/bytes, distance or another justified cost |
| Mechanism check | Evidence that prediction changed the intended decision |
| Robustness check | One omitted assumption most likely to change the ranking |

If you use mission time, report incomplete runs separately rather than discarding them or treating a timeout as a measured completion time. Coverage at a fixed time and completion proportion can show cases that never finish. If you report redundant coverage, define whether this counts repeated observations, repeated area or only avoidable overlap; discretization and sensor overlap can otherwise dominate it.

Report variation and effect sizes, not just means or a single attractive trajectory. For paired runs, analyze per-run differences and uncertainty using a method compatible with their distribution and sample size. Do not select a t-test by default without checking its assumptions. State how multiple exploratory comparisons are distinguished from the primary comparison.

Avoid unsupported environmental claims: shorter routes or fewer transmissions are proxies, not measured energy savings or lower emissions. Name the missing energy model and account for simulation-compute cost proportionately.
