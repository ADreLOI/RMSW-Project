\section{Forecast and protocol specification}\label{app:forecast}
Let $\boldsymbol u_k=\boldsymbol p_2(t_k)-\boldsymbol p_1(t_k)$ be the relative position in metres at a successful exchange. If $\ell<k$ is the preceding successful exchange, the relative-velocity estimate is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\boldsymbol v}_k=\frac{\boldsymbol u_k-\boldsymbol u_\ell}{t_k-t_\ell},\qquad
\hat d_{k+h}=\|\boldsymbol u_k+h\Delta\widehat{\boldsymbol v}_k\|_2.
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta=10$ s and velocity is in m/s. For the last five successful samples, ordinary least squares fits $\hat e(t)=\hat a+\hat b t$ to
\begin{equation}
e_j=r_j-\left[P_0-10n\log_{10}\left(\max(d_j,d_0)/d_0\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Residuals and intercept are in dB and slope in dB/s. Actual timestamps retain gaps. With known synthetic path-loss parameters, the trend forecast is
\begin{equation}
\hat r_{k+h}=P_0-10n\log_{10}\left(\max(\hat d_{k+h},d_0)/d_0\right)+\hat e(t_k+h\Delta).
\end{equation}
Persistence instead sets $\hat r_{k+h}=r_k$. The trend predictor uses persistence until five successful samples exist. Neither warns before a received sample. The forecast is positive when
\begin{equation}
\exists h\in\{1,2,3\}:\quad
\hat r_{k+h}<\theta+m\ \land\ \hat r_{k+h+1}<\theta+m.
\end{equation}
Thus four projected samples test a two-miss episode whose onset is within three future opportunities; they do not extend the onset horizon to four. The selected margin is $m=4$ dB for both methods. Only successful exchanges support reservation, and no future channel or slowdown draw is visible to either predictor.

Task IDs are row-major. Initial common knowledge includes both starting observations. Executing tasks remain fixed at replanning; only unstarted tasks are released. Paths minimize turns among shortest Manhattan routes and use horizontal-first ties. The initial heading has no turn cost. Movement continues through unused time within a tick after a waypoint. A current reported speed is held constant in each projected workload estimate. These deterministic choices apply to every online policy.

Messages use compact sorted-key UTF-8 JSON. A successful atomic session consists of two state messages, one versioned proposal and one acknowledgement. A failed radio opportunity transmits the two state messages without delivery; a budget-rejected offered batch transmits zero. Payload bytes exclude lower-layer headers. A completed mission stops immediately, with no final exchange, return voyage or upload. The model samples radio even for S1 solely to compare route-dependent link conditions; S1 performs no coordination.

Coverage is the mean final fraction across all runs, including completions and timeouts. Link availability is the mean per-run fraction of scheduled opportunities with $r_k\geq\theta$. For S1 this is a sampled, unused link diagnostic.
