\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and Rationale}
Cooperative seabed surveying requires vehicles to observe a target area and divide its work. Coverage path planning includes marine applications \cite{galceran2013coverage}. Yang et al. study multiple unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) surveying seabed regions of interest, with attention to region decomposition, travel and turns \cite{yang2023cooperative}. Marine multi-robot coordination also links communication constraints with task allocation \cite{martorell2024coordination}. Recent field-validated work plans USV bathymetric coverage, including energy-aware sweeps under currents \cite{zhao2024optimal,zhao2024energy}, and probabilistic seabed maps quantify bathymetric uncertainty \cite{torroba2022svgp}. These studies motivate our application, but observing every cell of an ideal grid does not establish the accuracy of a bathymetric map.

A fixed partition is a useful reference when workloads and vehicle progress are balanced. Our motivating case is an initially balanced assignment followed by an unexpected temporary slowdown. Online reassignment may redistribute unstarted work if vehicles can exchange current coverage and task state. Intermittent communication makes that information uncertain.

We ask whether reserving more work before a predicted outage improves this two-vehicle survey. An always-long queue could provide the same benefit, while additional commitments could delay reassignment. Route changes can also alter connectivity. The experiment therefore retains both fixed partitioning and a non-predictive long-queue control, and accepts null or adverse outcomes.

\subsection{Theory and Related Work}
Bramblett and Bezzo represent beliefs about disconnected robots and plan when robots should regroup \cite{bramblett2023epistemic}. Martorell-Torres et al. combine a marine coordination taxonomy with an acoustic-data-collection approach for heterogeneous surface and underwater robots \cite{martorell2024coordination}. Surveys of communication in multi-robot systems and of exploration under restricted communication identify intermittent connectivity as a central open problem \cite{gielis2022review,amigoni2017survey}. Periodic or planned rendezvous is a common remedy \cite{hollinger2010periodic,dasilva2024rendezvous}, including learned implicit rendezvous with public code \cite{tan2024ir2}. The frozen core contains no regrouping controller; a separately frozen extension adds the simplest pre-agreed meeting point. Gordon et al. incorporate estimated radio maps into communication-aware robot motion planning \cite{gordon2026communication}; our intervention changes task reservation capacity rather than selecting routes from radio-risk maps.

Prediction also has different roles across this literature. Gong et al. predict path variables to reduce transmissions during synchronized USV path following \cite{gong2022resource}. Here the target is a future radio-outage episode. Published consensus-based auctions provide an allocation reference \cite{choi2009auction}, but our deterministic greedy rule does not implement the consensus-based bundle algorithm's distributed agreement or inherit its guarantees. No external robotics implementation was adopted.

The model combines a log-distance signal form \cite{goldsmith2005pathloss} with a two-state burst process motivated by Gilbert \cite{gilbert1960burst}. Near-shore measurements for maritime robots found two-ray models adequate for line-of-sight 2.4/5 GHz loss and a mean change of about 10 dB between land and sea paths \cite{yamamoto2019rssi}. This makes our 0/$-10$ dB offsets plausible but does not calibrate them. Link-quality prediction ranges from Kalman-filtered path-loss models for mobile robots \cite{parasuraman2023rapid} to learned estimators \cite{cerar2021lqe,kanto2024lstm}. Persistence and ordinary least squares are standard forecasting components \cite{hyndman2021forecasting,nistLeastSquares}. The combined residual-signal and relative-motion warning is a study-specific construction. We compare reservation policies under one common allocator, without claiming superiority to published coverage or allocation systems.

\subsection{Research Problem, Aim, and Research Questions}
The aim is to evaluate whether short-horizon prediction of communication outages can improve online task allocation between two USVs conducting a cooperative seabed survey under intermittent boat-to-boat connectivity. The main question is: how does short-horizon outage prediction used for pre-outage task reservation affect mission completion time and redundant coverage relative to reactive resynchronization?
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.}]
\item Across fade-persistence conditions and balanced versus temporarily unequal progress, what is the effect of prediction-triggered reservation on completion time and redundant coverage relative to reactive resynchronization?
\item Does any benefit remain relative to the same larger reservation backlog without prediction, and what communication, computation, and memory costs accompany it?
\item How do persistence and signal-and-motion trend predictors compare in false alarms, missed outages, lead time, and mission outcomes when they use the same reservation action?
\end{enumerate}
These are the revised plan's three questions. S4-T was pre-specified for the primary comparison. Anticipation can help only when a warning is available and the reservation action improves the mission; temporal correlation alone does not ensure predictable fade onset. Fixed partitioning remains a strong reference.
