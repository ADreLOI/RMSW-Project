\subsection{Reservation control and warning quality}
S4-T showed no consistent improvement over S3. Its mean restricted-time difference from S3 was positive in all six conditions, ranging from $2.8$ to $196.3$ s. At $L=5$ without slowdown it was $+196.3$ s ($[0.7,583.0]$); with slowdown it was $+194.2$ s ($[-0.2,577.5]$). The trend/persistence comparison was also mixed: at $L=5$ S4-T was about $575$ s slower without slowdown and $570$ s slower with slowdown, while several other conditions differed only slightly. The complete paired contrasts, including uncertainty, are provided with the generated data.

On held-out fixed-route replay, the trend predictor matched 197 of 757 eligible outages, with 1299 false alerts: precision $0.132$, recall $0.260$, F1 $0.175$. Persistence matched 196, with 1280 false alerts: precision $0.133$, recall $0.259$, F1 $0.176$. Trend missed 560 eligible onsets and persistence missed 561. Forty early-boundary outages and 25 late-boundary alerts per predictor were excluded. Matched trend lead times were $10/20/30$ s in $80/59/58$ cases, versus $79/59/58$ for persistence; both medians were $20$ s. These are descriptive event counts, not independent trials or proof of forecast superiority.

In online missions, S4-T committed 6475 warning-triggered queue extensions; 1086 occurred one to three opportunities before an eligible outage. S4-P committed 5127, with 1007 in that window. At least one such commit preceded $443/538$ eligible S4-T onsets and $393/553$ S4-P onsets. These counts establish that warnings sometimes changed reservation capacity before an outage. They do not establish that the change improved a mission.

\subsection{Coverage and resource trade-offs}
The online long-queue and predictive methods produced redundant observations, including travel through already observed cells; S2's zero redundancy accompanies severe incomplete coverage. No duplicate task ownership or session-budget violation was detected. S2's mean link availability was $3.3\%$, with about 694 distance-related failed opportunities per run. For S3, S4-T and S4-P, mean availability was $73.5\%$, $72.4\%$ and $74.9\%$. Condition-specific availability and observed outage durations are in Appendix~\ref{app:condition-results}; final outage episodes may be truncated by stopping.

S1's mean missing-information fraction was $25.70\%$ before scheduled opportunities and $49.58\%$ at stopping, consistent with completing disjoint work without exchanging observations. Online mean pre-synchronization gaps were $1.10$--$1.62\%$ and stopping gaps $0.82$--$1.66\%$. Thus full true coverage does not imply that either boat holds the entire coverage record. Communication and work counts stop at completion or timeout.
