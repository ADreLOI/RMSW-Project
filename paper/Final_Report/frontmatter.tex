\unnumberedheading{Abstract}
Two unmanned surface vehicles can continue a survey during radio outages if each has work assigned in advance. We tested whether a warning based on recent signal and motion improves the timing of those assignments. Five policies surveyed a 240-cell grid under six progress and radio conditions, using 30 matched seeds per condition. Fixed partitioning completed all 180 missions. A three-task queue completed none. A twelve-task queue completed 158, compared with 156 for trend-triggered reservation and 162 for reservation based on the last received signal. A diagnostic intervention traced the twelve-task policy's failures to two exchanges near the first separation of the boats. Forcing the signal to fade at both exchanges stalled all 180 missions under this policy, whereas clearing both allowed all to finish. The predictive policies showed a similar pattern with exceptions. In a separately frozen extension with a rendezvous rule and fresh seeds, all policies completed. Trend-triggered reservation finished 108--175 s sooner on average than the short queue across conditions and 13--28 s later than the long queue. The evidence supports reserving a backlog and providing recovery in this synthetic survey geometry. The incremental value of predicting outages remains unestablished.
