\subsection{Data, Materials, Participants, or Other Sources}

The evidence will be generated by simulation; no human participants are involved. The core scenario contains two identical surface USVs in a known rectangular grid, where entering a cell records one survey observation. To make online reallocation meaningful, the scenario will include one controlled source of unequal progress, such as spatially varying traversal times or temporary speed reductions shared across paired runs. The vehicles exchange timestamped positions, local coverage state, and current task claims through a direct boat-to-boat link. A missed exchange leaves each vehicle with an outdated view of the other vehicle's work. An outage will be defined as the link remaining unavailable for a specified minimum duration within a prediction horizon. Communication severity will be operationalized primarily as mean outage duration; outage frequency and observed link availability will be recorded as descriptive checks. The predictor will use only recent link observations and relative motion available at decision time, never future simulator state. Each non-arbitrary motion, sensing, and communication parameter will be stored with its unit and justified from a cited source; the remaining synthetic assumptions will be labeled explicitly.

Each run will log true vehicle positions, surveyed cells, local coverage beliefs, link state, sent and delivered messages, predicted outages, task reservations, and target changes. These logs will provide mission completion time, coverage at timeout, redundant observations, distance traveled, and communication overhead in messages and bytes. Configurations, random seeds, software versions, and any reused code revision will be recorded. The main limitations are the simplified grid, idealized sensing footprint, and synthetic communication model: the study evaluates coordination behavior, not sonar reconstruction, hydrodynamics, collision avoidance, or a fully validated maritime channel.
