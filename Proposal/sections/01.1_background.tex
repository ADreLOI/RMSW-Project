\subsection{Background, Rationale, and Related Work}

Seabed mapping can be treated as a coverage problem: a vehicle must plan a path so that the relevant area, or volume, is observed by its sensors. Coverage path planning is a central problem in robotics and appears in many applications, including underwater vehicles used for image mosaicing and inspection \cite{galceran2013coverage}. In marine settings, coverage is not only a geometric problem. The useful quality of a survey also depends on sensing footprint, vehicle motion, positioning uncertainty, environmental disturbances, and the ability to exchange information during the mission.

Using multiple vehicles can improve survey efficiency because the mission area can be divided across the team. However, marine multi-robot systems introduce coordination problems that do not appear in the same way for a single vehicle. Prior work on marine multi-robot systems identifies communication, task division, task allocation, and coordination as tightly connected challenges, especially because marine communication links can be limited or intermittent \cite{martorell2024coordination}. If a team replans online, then the quality and freshness of shared information can affect which regions are assigned, which regions are revisited, and how quickly the team completes the mission.

The choice of vehicle and environmental model affects the realism and scope of the study. Surface vehicles can often be approximated as moving on a two-dimensional plane, while underwater vehicles may require depth or altitude decisions and must rely on more constrained underwater communication. Recent work on AUV trajectory planning distinguishes between planar 2D models, 2.5D bathymetric height-field models, and full 3D representations, noting that more detailed models can represent terrain clearance, visibility, and occlusion at higher computational and modelling cost \cite{almuzaini2026trajectory}. Underwater acoustic networking is also constrained by limited bandwidth, long and variable delays, Doppler effects, multipath, and environmental dependence \cite{alshawabka2026underwater}.

High-fidelity simulators such as Gazebo-based VRX can represent vessel dynamics, waves, wind, sensors, and other maritime effects \cite{bingham2019vrx}. Such platforms are useful when the research question concerns control, navigation, or vehicle behaviour in a realistic environment. For this project, the primary question concerns whether predicting short-term communication outages changes cooperative mapping performance. A controlled simulation can therefore be more appropriate for the main experiments, because it allows the communication and coordination mechanisms to be isolated and tested over repeated conditions. A more realistic 3D or AUV-inspired extension can still be considered if the simpler model cannot create a meaningful coordination challenge.
