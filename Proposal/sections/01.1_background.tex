\subsection{Background, Rationale, and Related Work}

Seabed mapping can be addressed as a coverage problem: a vehicle must plan a path so that the relevant area is observed by its sensors. Coverage path planning is a central problem in robotics and includes marine applications such as image mosaicing and inspection \cite{galceran2013coverage}. In a simplified survey model, coverage can be represented as cells observed by the vehicle, while more detailed bathymetric reconstruction can remain outside the scope of the experiment.

Using multiple vehicles can improve survey efficiency because the mission area can be split across the fleet. However, marine multi-robot systems introduce coordination problems. Prior work identifies communication, task division, task allocation, and coordination as closely connected challenges, especially because marine communication links can be limited or intermittent \cite{martorell2024coordination}. Related work on connectivity-aware multi-robot exploration also shows that communication quality can affect mission completion time and the allocation of robots to exploration or communication-support roles \cite{abuaisheh2022cara}.

In this project, communication is relevant only if the task allocation can change during the mission. With a fixed disjoint partition, each USV can continue surveying its assigned region even when disconnected, making communication mainly a monitoring issue. The coordination problem becomes more meaningful when progress is unequal, for example because some cells take longer to traverse or a vehicle is temporarily slowed. Then online reallocation can reduce idle time or delayed coverage, but stale task claims can also cause duplicated work. A controlled 2D simulation is therefore an appropriate starting point because it isolates this coordination mechanism before adding more realistic marine dynamics.
