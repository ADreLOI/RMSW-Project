\subsection{Research Design and Methodological Rationale}

We propose a controlled, quantitative simulation comparing three coordination strategies for two unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) surveying the same known two-dimensional area under intermittent direct communication. The strategies are a communication-agnostic fixed partition, reactive online coordination that resynchronizes after a link loss, and prediction-aware coordination that exchanges coverage and task state and reserves non-overlapping work before a predicted outage. Communication severity is varied primarily through the mean outage duration, while mission completion time is the primary outcome; redundant coverage and communication cost are secondary measures.

The motion and sensing models, online task allocator, scenario traces, update interval, and resource accounting will be identical for the reactive and prediction-aware strategies. Only the outage-handling policy will differ. Paired simulations can therefore isolate whether anticipating an outage changes task allocation and mission outcomes. Real-boat experiments would provide stronger external validity, but are not feasible within the available time and resources and would make communication conditions harder to control. Any realism extension will be limited to a targeted sensitivity check within the 2D model, such as a stronger motion disturbance or an alternative link-correlation assumption. Software tests will verify the implementation, while source-based parameter choices and sensitivity analysis will address model plausibility. Conclusions will remain limited to the modeled two-USV scenario rather than real-world navigation safety, channel performance, or seabed-map accuracy.
