\section{Introduction}

Marine robots are used in seabed mapping missions in areas where repeated human-operated surveys are expensive, slow, or risky. A team of vehicles can potentially complete such missions faster than a single vehicle, but only if the work is divided and updated continuously during execution. This makes robot coordination a central part of cooperative mapping.

This project investigates how intermittent connectivity affects cooperative seabed mapping. The study will start from a simplified 2D or 2.5D simulation. if the initial model is too limited, it could be extended toward 3D underwater motion. The main focus is coordination under communication loss, and not to reproduce a hydrodinamic model.
