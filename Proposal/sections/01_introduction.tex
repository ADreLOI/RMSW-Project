\section{Introduction}

Marine robots are used in seabed mapping missions in areas where repeated human-operated surveys are expensive, slow, or risky. A team of unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) can potentially complete such missions faster than a single vehicle by dividing the survey area and updating task assignments during execution. This makes robot coordination a central part of cooperative mapping, especially when the vehicles do not always share the same view of mission progress.

This project investigates how intermittent direct connectivity affects cooperative seabed mapping with two surface vehicles in a controlled two-dimensional survey scenario. The focus is not hydrodynamic modeling, sonar reconstruction, or detailed vehicle dynamics, but the coordination problem that appears when vehicles continue working with outdated information about coverage and task assignments.
