\section{Introduction}

Marine robots can support seabed mapping missions in areas where repeated human-operated surveys are expensive, slow, or risky. A single vehicle can perform such a mission by following a planned coverage path, but a team of vehicles can potentially reduce the mission time and increase robustness if the work is divided and updated during execution. This benefit depends on coordination. If the vehicles lose communication, each vehicle may continue from an outdated local view of the mission, including incomplete knowledge of already surveyed regions and stale task assignments.

This project investigates cooperative seabed mapping under intermittent connectivity. The proposed study will begin with a controlled two-dimensional or 2.5-dimensional simulation, where vehicles move over a survey area and build local coverage or bathymetric maps. If this model proves too limited for the research question, the project may extend the model toward a simplified three-dimensional underwater-vehicle setting, where vehicles can vary depth or altitude relative to the seabed. The main focus will remain on coordination under communication loss rather than on high-fidelity hydrodynamics.
