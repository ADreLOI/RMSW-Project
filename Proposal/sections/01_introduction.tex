\section{Introduction}

Marine robots are used to survey seabeds where repeated human-operated missions are expensive, slow, or risky. A team of unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) can potentially complete such missions faster than a single vehicle by dividing the survey area and, when progress differs, reallocating unfinished work. Dynamic reallocation requires sufficiently current information about each vehicle's progress.

This project investigates how intermittent direct boat-to-boat radio connectivity affects cooperative survey coverage by two USVs in a controlled two-dimensional seabed-survey scenario. The focus is not hydrodynamic modeling, sonar reconstruction, or detailed vehicle dynamics, but the coordination problem that appears when vehicles continue working with outdated information about coverage and task assignments.
